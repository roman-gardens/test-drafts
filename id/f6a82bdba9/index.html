Neoplatonic School Near the Areopagus

Author: Maureen Carroll
Contributor: Jane Millar
Published: 2021-04-21
Last modified: 2026-06-08

Dates

Second half of the 4th century CE to the late 6th century CE

Garden Description

Several houses with peristyle courtyards were built north of the Areopagus in the second half of the 4th century CE. These have been interpreted as schools of the Neoplatonic philosophers in which the school directors lived and students were instructed.

One of these buildings, the so-called "House C" or "Omega House", had two peristyle courtyards, of which the eastern and smallest was paved (Fig. 1). The house was assured of a constant supply of water, as it tapped into a spring higher up on the hill. The nymphaeum and semi-circular collecting basin on the southeast corner of the house were fed by this, the overflow being directed through a pipe to a well on the west side of the central, rectangular peristyle courtyard. The water taken from this well may have been used to water a garden, possibly one in the unpaved courtyard in which the well was situated (G on plan). A collection of marble statues, portrait busts, and reliefs from the 4th century BCE to the 3rd century CE was discarded in this and other wells of the house in the first half of the 6th century, possibly at the same time Justinian ordered that pagan schools should be closed down. The house was destroyed in 582/3 after a period of Christian use.
