Province Description (Italia)

Italia was not considered a province until the reign of Diocletian. However, according to Pliny's Naturalis Historia, the peninsula was divided into eleven administrative regions by Augustus around 7 BCE:

  • Regio I: Latium et Campania
    • Includes major sites in Roma, Pompeii, and Herculaneum
  • Regio II: Apulia et Calabria
  • Regio III: Lucania et Bruttium
  • Regio IV: Samnium
  • Regio V: Picenum
  • Regio VI: Umbria et Ager Gallicus
  • Regio VII: Etruria
  • Regio VIII: Aemilia
  • Regio IX: Liguria
  • Regio X: Venetia et Histria
  • Regio XI: Transpadana

Place Description (Latium et Campania)

Around 7 BCE, Latium et Campania were defined as Regio I of Italia by Augustus. This region includes major archaeological sites in Roma, Pompeii, and Herculaneum.

Place Description (Regio X Palatium)

In the republican period the Palatine had small but numerous gardens associated with well-appointed houses that were on this hill by the 1st century BC. Among these numerous residences were those of Lucius Crassus, famous for its six majestic nettle-trees (celtis australis) (Plin. N.H., 17,3-6) and Cicero’s domus, which offered the author “all the pleasures that gardens can give” (Ad Quinct. III,1,4). This orator’s house even included a Greek-style palaestra (laconicum) (Cic. ad Att. II,4,7), as well as an avenue (ambulatio) for promenades (CIL VI, 29774-29775). At the beginning of the Augustan period gardens had become almost as much a necessity for Romans as houses, and one must suppose that no domus of any importance lacked a peripheral garden or one planted within a peristyle.

Garden Description

Philostratus (Life of Apollonius of Tyana, 7.32) writes of the Adonea, a garden sacred to Adonis, in the Flavian Palace on the Palatine where Apollonius and Domitian met. It is also where the emperor had made a sacrifice to Minerva in the hall of Adonis that was overflowing with flowers in vases. It was proposed in the nineteenth century that this Adonea was in the area of the Vigna Barberini and that a fragment of the Severan Forma Urbis (FUR) partially depicts it. Recent excavations do not support such a location for the Adonea, and the marble fragment could represent the Severan Adonea that was in Trastevere. Flowers in vases, such as were part of the cult activities to Adonis, do not necessarily indicate a planted garden, although the marble fragment, with its symmetrical lines and rows of dots, likely render schematically formal garden plantings.
